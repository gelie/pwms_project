About PWMS

The Parliament Workflow Management System — one auditable place to track each parliamentary instrument through every stage of its lifecycle.

Why PWMS exists

Parliamentary instruments rarely move in a straight line. A bill is introduced, referred to a committee, opened for public comment, deliberated, reported on, debated, sent to the National Council of Provinces and finally submitted for assent — and at each of those steps somebody different is responsible for the next action. The same is true of delegation reports, international resolutions and international agreements.

PWMS keeps that movement in one system, for the Parliament of the Republic of South Africa. Every instrument has a type, a current stage, a named owner and a deadline, so three questions can always be answered from a single record: where is this, who has it, and what has happened to it so far?

What it tracks

Four types of instrument today, each following the procedural stages set out in its business requirements:

  • Delegation reports
    Official parliamentary delegations, including the delegates who took part and the resolutions they adopted.
    Awaiting PGIR approval → Submitted for tabling → Tabled and referred to Committee → Closed – House approved
  • International resolutions
    Resolutions adopted by delegations and the work to implement them. A resolution is nested under the delegation report it came from.
    Captured → Assigned → In Progress → Implemented → Closed
  • International agreements
    Agreements arising from South Africa's international obligations, from tabling through committee scrutiny to adoption by the House.
    Submitted for tabling → Agreement Tabled – referred to Committee → Committee considering and processing → Committee submitted report for tabling → House adopted – referred to Department → Closed – House approved
  • Bills
    Legislation from introduction to assent, including public participation, committee deliberation, the NCOP stage and mediation.
    Introduced → Referred to Committee → Public Participation → Committee Deliberation → Committee Report → House Debate and Voting → NCOP Consideration → Awaiting Presidential Assent → Signed into Law

Every stage also carries a citizen-facing public status, so a detailed internal machine can publish a small, stable set of statuses — Introduced, Under Parliamentary Consideration, Signed into Law — without exposing each committee step along the way.

How an instrument moves

Stages and transitions

Each instrument follows a workflow type — a small state machine of stages joined by the transitions allowed between them. PWMS offers only the transitions valid from the current stage, and can insist that evidence exists first: a document attached, an ATC update published, no unanswered referrals, or all related sub-workflows closed.

Deadlines and referrals

Every instrument carries an owner, an optional assignee, a deadline and a priority. Work can be referred to a committee or another group with a response deadline attached; an unanswered referral can hold an instrument back, and an expired deadline is flagged as overdue rather than quietly missed.

Who may do what

Access follows your role

What you can see and do is decided by the groups you belong to, the role you hold in them, and the stage the instrument has reached — so a committee might be able to edit a draft but only read it once it has been tabled. Creating an instrument is restricted to the roles its workflow type names.

The people named on a record

The owner of an instrument can always view, edit and delete it, and whoever the work is assigned to can always open it — an assignment is a queue, not a promotion. Access flows down from a parent instrument to the ones nested inside it, never the other way round.

A record you can trust

Nothing is overwritten quietly. Every create, edit and delete of an instrument is recorded with the acting user and a before/after change, and every stage change is logged as a transition with its timestamp, user and any comment.

Alongside those, an instrument keeps an append-only event timeline for business facts such as a report document being attached or an ATC update being published. Events are evidence: they are never edited, and a correction is added as a new event rather than written over the old one.

Finding your work

Your dashboard gathers what needs attention — work assigned to you, deadlines falling due, anything overdue, and referrals your committees have yet to answer — alongside a breakdown of the instruments within your reach. The workflow register lists everything you can see in one place, filterable by type, stage and priority, while each type keeps its own register and detail page with its participants, sub-workflows, referrals and history.

Getting access and help

Access is not requested through the system itself: it follows your group membership. An administrator records you as a member of a group with a role, and what you can see and do follows from that — so when something is missing, the first question is usually whether your membership is current.

For a problem with PWMS, or to be added to a group or role, see Contact.

PWMS is in active development (version 0.1.0). The workflow engine, access control and audit trail are in place, and referral deadline reminders are emailed; broader notifications, document storage and further instruments are on the roadmap.
